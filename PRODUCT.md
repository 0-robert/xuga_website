# PRODUCT.md — XUGA Towel Tales

register: brand

## What this is

Towel Tales is a storytelling microsite for XUGA, a Malta-based, student-led
brand that upcycles used beach towels into one-of-a-kind clothing and
accessories. Every garment carries a QR code. Scanning it opens a story page
where the original towel, given a voice, tells how it became the thing the
buyer is holding.

The design IS the product here. The site's job is to make a buyer feel their
piece is singular and worth keeping.

## Users

- **The buyer**, scanning the QR on a garment they just bought or were gifted.
  Mostly on a phone, often outdoors, curious, expecting a small delight.
- **The gift-giver**, who wants the recipient to feel the piece is personal.
- **The market visitor**, scanning at a pop-up stall to decide whether to buy.
- **XUGA's own team** (students), who add a new story for every piece made and
  need that to take minutes, not an afternoon.

## Brand and tone

Warm, handmade, sunlit, Maltese. Playful but never gimmicky. Sustainability is
shown through real provenance and honest numbers, not slogans.

The towels speak in a **warm and sentimental** voice: fond, gentle, telling
their story like a real memory. Emotional without being saccharine. The voice
must never read as AI-generated. Every story is run through the humanizer
process before it ships.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: clean, gridded, soulless.
- Fast-fashion gloss: glossy studio product shots, infinite identical cards.
- Greenwashing: vague "eco" badges with no substance.
- AI-quirk mascot voice: relentless one-liners, "plot twist", forced punch.

## Strategic principles

1. **The garment sells itself; the QR is a delight, not a dependency.** Pages
   must charm, never gate.
2. **Adding a story must stay effortless.** One Markdown file, no code.
3. **Honesty over hype.** Fuzzy provenance is framed as "rescued from", eco
   figures are labelled "estimated".
4. **Every piece is one of one.** The design should make that felt, not stated.
